The problem it solves

Model railroad planning is full of numbers that lie by omission. A grade that fits your benchwork might stall your longest train. A 24-inch-radius helix at 2% rises barely 3 inches per turn - not enough to clear the train below it once you count roadbed. And "how many 40-foot cars fit in this siding" is a division nobody does until the yard is full. Railmath puts all four answers on one page, with the rules of thumb labeled as rules of thumb.

Scale speed that times right

Convert prototype mph to layout speed. At scale speed, a car passes a point in the same seconds as the real thing - pure math, no scale table.

Helix clearance check

Radius, grade and climb in; turns, track length and rise-per-turn out - flagged against the clearance your tallest equipment needs.

Honest labels

NMRA scale ratios are standards; the 2% grade band and coupler overhang are community guidance. Railmath never blurs the two.
